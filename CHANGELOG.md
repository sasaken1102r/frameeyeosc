# Changelog

## Unreleased

The eye cameras' left and right were swapped since 0.7.2 (widening, squint, pupils, calibration messages); fixed, and saved calibrations are converted once. With the camera, a narrowed eye isn't sent closed, and an open eye that Valve's values read low isn't sent drowsy (without the camera, an eye read nearly open, or a little low far down, goes out open; `lid_open_snap` sets from where). The eye fit starts with the dashboard open; fits and calibrations leave local records.

日本語: 0.7.2 から目のカメラの左右が逆になっていて、見開き・細め・瞳孔と、校正の目ごとのメッセージがもう片方の目に付いていました。正しい目に付くように直し、保存してある校正は一度だけ入れ替えます。目のカメラがあるときは、細めた目を閉じた目として送らず、Valve の値が低く読む開いた目も眠そうな目で送らないようにしました（カメラがないときは、ほぼ開いた値や、深く下を見て少し低い値も開いた目にします。どこからかは `lid_open_snap` で決められます）。目合わせはダッシュボードを開いたまますぐ始まり、校正と目合わせのたびに小さな記録を Frame の中に残します。

- eyecam-rec tells the two cameras apart by where their frames sit in memory, and called the lower-address camera the left eye. It is the right eye's: with one eye closed at a time, Valve's openness went to 0 on the closed eye while eyecam said the other one was closed. The cameras' pictures are processed exactly as before; only the eye they are given to changes. The camera's own idea of looking up or down now also uses Valve's gaze of the same eye.
- `~/.config/eyecam/calib.json` is converted once at start (left and right values, the eye that failed, the warnings) and gets `"eyes": "anatomical"`; the file as it was is kept first as `calib.json.pre-anatomical` (one already there is not overwritten). `calib_result.json` gets the mark too. Older `calib_result.json` files (the panel's last calibration after a restart, the widening history) are read the other way round. Going back to an older version after this reads the converted calib.json the wrong way round again, and that version saves it without the mark, so a later upgrade exchanges it a second time: after going back and upgrading again, redo the eye-camera calibration (or, with eyecam stopped, copy `calib.json.pre-anatomical` back to `calib.json` first).
- `status.json`'s per-eye fields (`pupil_l` / `pupil_r`, `cam_fps`, `calib_failed_eye`, `last_calib`) and the live shared memory's eyes now follow the real eyes.
- Recordings: `meta.txt` gets `eye_files=anatomical` and `eye_L.raw` holds the left eye. `eyecam-rec --replay` reads older recordings the other way round, so each camera's frames get the same processing as before; `convert.py` and `fix_swap.py` know the new key. `--swap` only renames the eyes: each camera's picture is flipped (or not) as its own, and `meta.txt`'s `upside_down_eye` names the file that is stored upside down (`none` with one camera streaming).
- Eyelids with the eye cameras: Valve's values often read an eye that is only narrowed (a squint, or one eye squinting along while the other is held shut) as closed, and it went out closed. Now, when Valve's values have read an eye as closing for longer than a blink (0.3 s in all, or 8 of the camera's frames if that is longer) while the camera sees it open, that eye takes the camera's eyelid until the two agree again for 0.2 s; it is then not pulled shut with the other eye either. Blinks are shorter, so they still close from Valve's values, and the blink sync still closes both eyes when Valve's values catch a blink in one eye only. An eye the camera sees closed goes out closed even when Valve's values read it open. Where the camera sees an eye widened and Valve's values read it at least two thirds open, the camera's widening shows (before, only from relaxed open up, so one eye could stay unwidened). Without the camera's values for an eye, nothing changes. `frameeyeosc --replay` says how often each eye's eyelid came from the camera this way.
- New setting `camera_lid_floor` (`--camera-lid-floor`, 0 to 0.75, off (0) by default, 0.25 for example; "Lowest eyelid when narrowed" on the Eyelids tab): while the eye camera sees an eye open, its eyelid is sent no lower than this. In a hard squint the camera's own eyelid goes nearly to 0, which looks closed on an avatar; now it shows narrowed and the squint parameter carries the rest. Blinks (Valve's values closing within the grace period) and an eye the camera sees closed still reach 0, and lid_sync doesn't pull a held eye below it. Eyes without the camera's values are unchanged.
- Eyelids with the eye cameras, a jump to 1.000: on SteamOS 0.4.3 Valve's values read a relaxed open eye as 1.000, which says only that it is open. An eye held half closed jumped to relaxed open for the 2-3 samples its reading went to 1.000, and back (the "shaking" eye of a 16-minute VRChat recording). Now, while the camera sees that eye clearly narrowed (its eyelid below 0.6, part of the way up to 0.7), such a jump is lowered to the camera's eyelid: fully for the first 0.1 s of a reading at 1.000 (readings from 0.90 up count part of the way), not at all after 0.2 s. It only ever lowers, so an open eye, also one that reads 1.000 for long (looking down, say), goes out as before. Replayed, eyelids that jumped up by 0.12 or more and came back within 0.1 s went from 84 / 96 to 68 / 42 (left / right). `frameeyeosc --replay` counts these as "at 1.000". Eyes without the camera's values are unchanged.
- The camera's widening no longer switches on and off: it fades in over the camera's first 0.05 above relaxed open, and, where Valve's values read the eye lower, over 0.5 to 0.6 of Valve's eyelid. A camera eyelid wobbling around relaxed open (0.750 / 0.773) had made a 0.6 eyelid jump to 0.74 and back.
- Widening in one eye only is halved: with both eyes' camera values, neither eye's eyelid goes further above relaxed open than the mean of the two cameras' widening. Widening in both eyes shows as before. In that recording about a fifth of the widen stretches were one eye only, the other eye's camera seeing none; replayed, they went from 22 / 27 to 12 / 10.
- Eyelids, an open eye read low: on SteamOS 0.4.x Valve's values often read an open eye at 0.6-0.95 (mostly looking down, at times for seconds straight ahead, mostly the right eye). Through an eye fit whose open reading is 1.000, a reading of 0.85 went out at about 0.55, and the avatar looked drowsy. With the eye cameras: while the camera sees an eye open (not closed) and Valve's values read it below relaxed open but not closing (at or above 0.25, or `blink_sync_below` if that is higher), its eyelid goes out no lower than the camera's, at most relaxed open. It only ever raises, so a squint the camera sees, blinks and winks keep Valve's values. Replayed on two VRChat recordings (7 and 16 minutes), an eye the camera saw open went out below 0.7 in 0.3% / 0.5% and 0.2% / 0.1% of the samples (left / right; were 19.5% / 17.8% and 5.2% / 11.4%), and looking more than 15° down in 0.7% / 1.3% and 1.1% / 0.6% (were 56% / 46% and 37% / 46%). Blinks fully closed and how long they stay closed, flicker and narrowed eyes are as before. `frameeyeosc --replay` counts these as "raised to it". The camera's widening on such an eye still goes by Valve's eyelid before the raising, so it doesn't let all of the widening through where Valve's values read the eye well below relaxed open.
- New setting `lid_open_snap` (`--lid-open-snap`, 0 to 0.75 in steps of 0.01, 0.53 by default, 0.75 = off; "Treat nearly open as open" on the Eyelids tab's "Fine-tune" page, shown there as mark ⑤ on the eyelid bars, a blue dashed line with a light band up to where it reaches 0.75, faded for an eye the cameras read), on the eyelid as it is sent (VRCFT; for a fitted eye before its scale, so a fine-tune like 0.9 still shows on top, for an eye without a fit after the scale that maps it): for an eye without the eye camera's eyelid, an eyelid at least this open eases smoothly up to a normally open eye (0.75), reached halfway from this value. The default is where a reading of 0.80 lands through an eye fit that reads 1.000 open (0.525-0.536 for the owner's two eyes; without a fit, at the default lid marks, 0.51), so on SteamOS 0.4.3 readings from 0.80 bend up and 0.90 and above go out relaxed open. Widening and anything below the value are unchanged. Test builds had it as a share of the open reading (0.70-1.00); such a value in config.json (above 0.75) is converted once (0.80 -> 0.53, 1.00 -> 0.75).
- Without the eye camera's eyelid, an eye looking further than 17° down is expected to read less open: 0.02 less per degree beyond 17° (no lower than half its straight-ahead reading), where the fit's 15° down reading used to hold. Valve's values read an open eye lower the further down it looks, and 24° down an open eye that read 0.82 went out half closed; now it goes out about relaxed open. Up to 17° down nothing changes. 17° and 0.02 come from one owner's two recordings; no setting.
- Replayed on two VRChat recordings (7 and 16 minutes) without the camera columns, an open eye went out below 0.7 in 9.8% / 6.9% and 1.8% / 4.4% of the samples (were 19.5% / 19.5% and 5.3% / 14.3%; the 17° rule alone 13.4% / 15.8% and 2.0% / 11.4%); looking more than 15° down 28% / 23% and 13% / 19% (were 56% / 46% and 38% / 48%). The cost: a narrowed eye that Valve's values read at 0.80-0.90 goes out more open (narrowed eyes sent at 0.7 or more: 70% / 35% -> 85% / 56% and 14% / 24% -> 18% / 48%). Both-eye blinks fully closed: 28 of 52 on the 7-minute recording (29 before), 54 of 91 on the other as before.
- The developer eye recording on the Advanced tab shows its steps as chips under the step number, as the eye-camera calibration does: done ones with a check, the one now in the accent, then what comes next, as many as fit, with "…" and "+N more" for the rest. They come from eyecam's protocol file (`~/.local/lib/eyecam/protocol_<name>.txt`); when it can't be read, only the step number shows, as before. `--fake-eyecam recording:LABEL` follows eyecam's real protocol (17 steps) and shows them.
- frameeyeosc's eye log (`frameeyeosc --record`, the panel's "Eye log") gets a last column `cam_eyes_anatomical`. `frameeyeosc --replay` reads a recording with eye-camera columns but without it (0.7.2 to 0.7.5) with `cam_l_*` and `cam_r_*` exchanged, and says so.
- Eye fit: it starts as soon as "Fit my eyes" or "Re-center only" is pressed, with the dashboard open; there is no "close the dashboard to start" wait and no 1-minute timeout. The dots are 0.9 m away (were 2 m), nearer than the open dashboard (about 1.35 m), so they show above it, and each eye's own sideways angle to a dot is worked out for 0.9 m. The first dot waits one second longer before it is measured, while the eyes come off the button. Meanwhile the panel shows only "Look at the dot" and a big "Stop".
- The fit stops on "Stop", on another tab, when the dashboard shows another page for half a second, and when the dashboard is opened after it was closed (closing it halfway doesn't stop it). The tab says why ("Stopped", "Stopped: you went to another tab or page", "Stopped: the dashboard was opened") and "Nothing was changed"; a stopped fit is not shown as a red failure.
- The Advanced tab has four pages under a switch at the top: "Version", "Having trouble", "Debug tools" and "Files". The page opened last is opened again, also after the panel restarts (kept in `~/.local/state/frameeyeosc/panel.json`, not in `config.json`).
- Records: each eye-camera calibration started from the panel and each eye fit (also the re-center run when the headset is put on) leaves a folder in `~/.local/state/frameeyeosc/reports/`; the newest 10 are kept. In it: `summary.json` (what ran, how it ended and why, the versions, the diagnostic code, the conditions), `status.jsonl` (frameeyeosc's and eyecam's status once a second), `logs.txt` (the journals of frameeyeosc, the panel and eyecam and Valve's eye-tracker log around it), for calibrations `calib_result.json` (the numbers), and `report.txt`. No eye images are kept, and nothing is sent anywhere. "Having trouble" lists the newest three and opens them; a failed fit or calibration links to its record. Over SSH: `frameeyeosc-panel --report latest`, `--report list` or `--report <folder>`. A run stopped by hand is shown as "Stopped" in grey, and one still going when the panel exits is kept as stopped.
- The panel draws its arrows, chevrons and marks (‹ › → ♪ ✓ and the like) as lines instead of font characters; the fold and scroll buttons are line chevrons.
- The Eyelids tab has three pages under a switch at the top, like the Advanced tab: "Look" (where the eyelids come from, how easily widening shows, eyelid smoothing, syncing both lids), "Blinks & squint" (the blink hold, blinking both eyes together, the lowest eyelid when narrowed) and "Fine-tune" (what "More" used to open: the auto calibration, eye scales, the openness marks, the smoothing values). The page opened last is opened again, also after the panel restarts (`lids_page` in `panel.json`). On SteamOS 0.4.3 without the eye cameras, the greyed widening slider and the box under it are now one line, "Valve's values alone can't widen", with the button to the Eye cameras tab. The Eye cameras tab's "Eyelids" button opens "Look". `frameeyeosc-panel --lids-page look|blinks|fine` picks the page for `--dump-png` (`--lid-marks` is `fine`).

## 0.7.5 (2026-10-06)

Eye-camera calibration works when the Frame's eye cameras deliver fewer than 90 frames a second (as few as 15 on some headsets), and the panel shows the camera's frame rate.

日本語: Frame の目のカメラが毎秒 90 枚より少ない（ヘッドセットによっては 15 枚）ときでも、目のカメラの校正が通るようにしました。パネルにカメラの毎秒の枚数を出します。

- Valve's eye tracker sets the eye cameras' frame rate itself: 90 at most, following the display refresh rate down to 80 or 72, and on some headsets only about 15 (cause unknown). The calibration needed a fixed number of frames (90 in the relaxed step, about 5.4 s), so at 15 a second it could never pass: `[L 81/81, R 81/81, 90 needed]` with the pupil seen in every frame (#23). The minimum is now the largest of a share of the frames the step delivered, about 1 second's worth at the measured rate and a small floor, and the message says the real number; at 90 a second the numbers are the same as before. A calibration that fails at under 60 frames a second adds "the eye cameras deliver only N frames a second".
- Everything eyecam-rec's live processing timed in frames (eyelid holds, the 100 ms before widening counts, medians, the 30-second baseline, the 60-second follow) is now timed in seconds at the measured rate. At 90 frames a second the output is byte for byte the same as before. Replaying the developer's recordings thinned to 72, 45, 18 and 15 frames a second, all 14 wear calibrations pass and widening, squint and pupils come out much as at 90; at 72 Hz it was also checked on a headset. A real 15-frame headset hasn't been tried yet.
- The search for the eye-camera video counts a slot as live from 3 refreshes a second (was 5), so 15-frame cameras are found.
- frameeyeosc uses a camera value for up to two of that camera's frame intervals (133 ms at 15 a second; still 100 ms at 90), so slow cameras don't flip between the camera's and Valve's eyelids.
- `status.json` has `cam_fps` (`[left, right]`, frames a second over the last 2 seconds while locked) and `last_calib.fps`. In `calib_result.json`, `thresholds` now gives each eye's numbers as `[left, right]` plus the `rule` (format change).
- The panel shows the camera rate on the Diagnostics page ("Camera N /s", red under 60) and in the setup's step 3 ("arriving (15 /s, low)"). `--fake-eyecam …:camfps=N`.
- For development: `eyecam-rec --replay --resample FPS`, `--replay --calib wear|user`, `--fake-camfps N`.

## 0.7.4 (2026-10-05)

The eye-camera setup no longer gets stuck at "not seen" for both eyes when the headset's proximity sensor reads low while worn. A new Diagnostics page shows, in one screenshot, what is going on, and the Advanced tab becomes one scrolling page with sections.

日本語: HMD の近接センサーがかぶっていても低い値になる人で、目のカメラの準備が「両目とも見えてないよ」のまま止まらないようにしました。新しい「診断」ページで、スクショ 1 枚で状況が分かります。「詳細」タブは見出しつきの 1 枚のスクロールページになりました。

- eyecam-rec looks for the eye-camera video whether or not the proximity sensor says the headset is worn (every second when it does, every 2 seconds otherwise). Before, it only looked once the sensor read above `--prox-min` (20) for a second, and on some faces it never does: on the developer's own headset it read 0 to 3 while worn on one day, and 20 to 42 on others, so the setup waited forever while Valve's eye tracking worked. With the cameras off nothing changes in memory and a look ends at once; idle CPU stays about 0.5 % of a core. The stale-buffer check (fetch the buffers again after 2 minutes worn with no change) now also counts the headset as worn while Valve's eye tracker publishes fresh samples (its shared memory is only read).
- `status.json` says why the video isn't found: `search` is `not_worn` (the sensor says not worn, Valve's eye tracker publishes nothing and nothing was found), `no_video` (worn, but the cameras write nothing: is eye tracking on in SteamVR?), `one_eye`, or `""`. It also has `prox_min`, `search_detail` (candidates, refreshes per second, slots, both eyes, where the look stopped, changed blocks), `last_calib` (the last wear calibration's time, result, pupil frames, pupil position and search window, from `calib_result.json` when this run hasn't calibrated) and `last_error` / `last_error_en` / `last_error_unix`. `calib_result.json` gets `failed_eye`.
- frameeyeosc's own `status.json` has `last_error` (`text`, `time`): the last problem it logged (eye data unreadable, name lookup, sending, Steam Link not found, files it couldn't write). It stays after the problem is gone.
- The panel's eye-camera setup says "Camera video: arriving" only while frames flow (before, it only meant the tool had handed over the buffers), and under the steps it says why the eyes aren't seen, with the proximity reading, plus a "Diagnostics" button. The usual eye-camera page says the same at the bottom.
- New Diagnostics page (Advanced tab, "Having trouble", "Open"): versions (frameeyeosc, SteamOS, the eye-camera tool's checksum), eye data (Valve's rate and misses, send rate and drops, the 0.4.3 openness cap, where the eyelids come from, frameeyeosc's last error), the eye cameras (state, the last search, changed blocks, proximity, pupils seen, processing time) and the last calibration. At the top a short diagnostic code such as `NW·P3·B0·G1·C1R·F1` to quote in a reply; panel/README.md explains it. A screenshot of this page can show your LAN IP (a fixed target) and file paths.
- The Advanced tab is one page with sections (Version, Having trouble, Debug tools, Files and process, Developer). When it doesn't fit, the thumbstick or touchpad scrolls it, as do ▲ / ▼ on the right. In English, the old "Diagnostics" section is now "Debug tools".

## 0.7.3 (2026-10-05)

Fewer eye-camera calibrations failing with "the pupil wasn't seen": the pupil search now fits faces whose eyes sit further toward the nose, and a calibration where only one eye fails still goes through. The panel says per eye whether the pupil is seen, and checks for updates every hour.

日本語: 目のカメラの校正が「瞳が見えなかった」で失敗しにくくなりました。目が鼻寄りに写る顔でも瞳を探せるようにし、片目だけ失敗したときも校正が通ります。パネルは目ごとに瞳が見えているかを出し、新しい版の確認を 1 時間に 1 回にしました。

- eyecam-rec finds the pupil in more faces. The search window's nose-side edge was fixed at x = 186, fitted to one face; it is now worked out for each wear from where the dark area beside the nose ends (100 to 186), and a dark blob touching that edge is kept when a leftward ray finds the pupil's rim (shadows still drop out). Up to three candidates are tried instead of one, and after 10 frames without a pupil, or when a calibration starts, it forgets where the pupil was last, so one bad frame no longer keeps it looking in the wrong place. A pupil found without that hint must also look like an open eye's (size, darkness, how much is visible), so eyelashes and the eyelid line aren't taken for the pupil while the eye closes. On the 14 calibrations recorded so far (28 eyes) nothing got worse (pupil seen in relaxed eyes 97.9 % → 98.0 %, the eyelid values within 0.002); with the eyes shifted 30 to 50 px toward the nose, every eye now has at least 427 of 486 frames with a pupil, where before some had none.
- A calibration checks both eyes before saying what failed, and says so per eye: the pupil wasn't seen (with the counts, e.g. `[L 12/486, R 30/486, 90 needed]`) or the upper eyelid line wasn't found. When only one eye fails, the calibration still goes through, as it already did when only the widening couldn't be measured: the good eye gets its new values, the other keeps its previous ones (or, the first time or when that eye failed last time too, provisional values from the other eye and earlier calibrations), and the message and the panel say which. `status.json` gives the eye in `calib_failed_eye` (`"L"`, `"R"`, `"LR"` or `""`).
- `status.json` has `pupil_l` and `pupil_r`: the share of the last 2 seconds' frames in which that eye's pupil was found (null while the live processing is off or that eye's video has stopped). The panel's setup checklist uses them to tell, per eye, "Seen", "Video is coming but the pupil isn't found" (with a hint to put the headset on again) and "Not seen".
- Each calibration's `calib_result.json` carries diagnostics per eye and step (where the search window was, where the pupil sat, why frames had no pupil), so a failure can be traced without eye video.
- The panel checks for a new release at start and every hour, instead of at most once a day (frame-updater 0.3.0).

## 0.7.2 (2026-10-05)

Optional support for the Frame's eye cameras: after a one-time setup on the panel's new "Eye cameras" tab, widened eyes come through again on SteamOS 0.4.3, and squints and pupil size are sent too. The eyelid look settings are gathered on the Eyelids tab, and the Advanced tab gets a version history.

日本語: Frame の目のカメラに対応（使うかどうかは自由）。パネルの新しい「目のカメラ」タブで一度だけ準備すると、SteamOS 0.4.3 でも見開きが届き、目を細めた動きと瞳孔の大きさも送れます。まぶたの見た目の調整は「まぶた」タブにまとめ、「詳細」タブに更新履歴を足しました。

- The release carries eyecam, an eye-camera tool (`tools/eyecam`, a copy of its own branch): `eyecam-rec` reads the infrared eye cameras' video while SteamVR's eye tracking runs and works out each eye's openness, widening, squint and pupil size about 90 times a second; `eyecam-grab` is the small helper that hands it the camera buffers. `install.sh` installs them, with `install_grab.sh` and the recording protocols, to `~/.local/lib/eyecam` (each file written next to the old one and renamed over it, so a running one is replaced safely) and enables and starts `eyecam.service`, a user service, on its first install; later updates restart it only if it is running, and one turned off (`systemctl --user disable --now eyecam`) stays off. The release notes give `eyecam-grab`'s sha256, and the Konsole the panel opens for `install_grab.sh` shows the installed file's above the command, to compare before typing the password. Until the eye cameras are set up it only waits, and nothing else changes. Calibrations keep no eye video (except in eyecam's developer mode, `"dev": true` in `~/.config/eyecam/settings.json`): each leaves only small text files, about 1 MB, in `~/eyecam/calib_…/`. `--uninstall` removes eyecam and `--purge` its settings and calibration too (`~/.config/eyecam`); the files in `~/eyecam` and the root-owned `/home/.eyecam` are left, and it prints how to remove the latter. `install.sh` never runs sudo (`FRAMEEYEOSC_NO_SYSTEMCTL=1` skips its systemctl calls, for trying it with another `HOME`).
- frameeyeosc mixes in eyecam-rec's values (`$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/eyecam/live`, read only), each eye on its own. An eye whose camera values are fresh (at most 100 ms old) and that has a baseline for this wear (a calibration, or the relaxed eyes eyecam-rec learns by itself in about 35 seconds of wearing) takes its eyelid from the camera from relaxed open up, so widening comes through even where Valve's openness tops out at 1.0 (SteamOS 0.4.3: the cap now applies only to eyes without the camera). Closing, half-closed eyes and blinks stay Valve's. VRChat mode also sends squints (`v2/EyeSquintLeft` / `EyeSquintRight` / `EyeSquint`, once the optional user calibration is done; 0 once when they stop) and pupil size (`v2/PupilDilation`, `v2/PupilDiameterLeft` / `PupilDiameterRight` / `PupilDiameter`). `camera_lids` (on by default, `--no-camera-lids`) turns it all off (and has eyecam-rec stop working out eye values from the video: frameeyeosc sends `live off` on its control socket whenever eyecam-rec is idle and still processing, so also after it restarts; the page's calibration buttons are greyed meanwhile), and `--eyecam-live PATH` reads another file. Without eyecam running nothing changes. The status file says what it uses (`camera`) and what went out (`sent.squint`, `sent.pupil_dilation`).
- In LiveLink mode, the eye cameras' pupil size goes straight to VRChat on the same PC (port 9000, with `prefix`, with each Live Link packet), since the LiveLink module carries no pupils. "Send pupils straight to VRChat" on the Output tab (`pupils_to_vrchat`, `--no-pupils-to-vrchat`; on by default) turns it off; the status file shows where they go (`pupil_target`).
- Pupils for avatars that take them bit-packed: "How the avatar takes pupils" on the Output tab (`pupil_bits`, `--pupil-bits`: "Float", or 1 to 4 bits) also sends the dilation as `v2/PupilDilation1`, `2`, `4`, `8` bools, worked out as VRCFaceTracking's binary parameters do, next to the float, wherever the pupils go to VRChat (VRChat mode, and LiveLink mode with "Send pupils straight to VRChat"). Only the bits that changed go out, and all of them once a second. It is one value for every avatar: the headset can't see the avatar's parameters (VRChat's OSCQuery only answers on the PC itself). The row shows with the eye cameras, greyed while the pupils don't go straight to VRChat.
- The panel's "Eye cameras" tab, shown while eyecam runs. Until the cameras are set up it is a checklist that moves on by itself: (1) set a SteamOS password, only if there is none (checked with `steamos-passwd --has-password`, which only reads); (2) install the tool: "Open in Konsole" opens Konsole with `sudo ~/.local/lib/eyecam/install_grab.sh` typed in, and you press Enter and type the password (the panel never runs sudo itself); (3) an 18-second calibration that learns your eye movements (only with both eyes seen; if only the widening couldn't be measured, it can carry on with standard values); (4) done. Then the tab shows what drives the eyelids now, "Eyelids from the eye cameras" (`camera_lids`), "Calibrate the cameras (18 s)" for when something feels off and the optional "User calibration (once)" for squints, each in the same card with the steps, the seconds left and Stop. Until the setup is done the left column shows what to do next, and right after it "Eye cameras: ready". Step (2) comes back whenever the tool is missing. - The panel's left column shows how many samples a second the eye tracker delivers ("Eye data 90 /s"), marked "low" in red below 60. frameeyeosc reports it in the status file (`tracker_rate`, from a second after tracking starts).
- `EyeTrackingActive` can be sent as a float (1.0 / 0.0) or not at all, for avatars that declare it as a float and stop tracking on a bool: "EyeTrackingActive type" on the Basic tab, `eye_tracking_active` (`"bool"` by default, `"float"`, `"off"`), `--eye-tracking-active`. "Off" also skips the one-time "not active" on pausing or losing tracking. ETVR mode never sends it, as before.
- frameeyeosc no longer exits when it can't send, for example with a fixed target PC while Wi-Fi isn't up yet after boot ("Network is unreachable"). It logs once when sending starts failing, with the reason, and once when it works again, and keeps trying. Eye tracking, the status file and the eye fit keep working meanwhile. Before, it exited and systemd restarted it every 5 seconds.

## 0.5.0 (2026-09-28)

Fitting the eyes to you, for when the avatar's eyes look a little off (for example looking too far down, or eyelids that close when you look down).

- The panel has a new "Eye fit" tab with one button. After you close the dashboard, a dot appears straight ahead, up, down, left and right, 2.5 seconds each, and then it asks you to close your eyes for 3 seconds. It takes about 20 seconds. The result shows on the tab, with "Fit again" and "Reset". "Re-center only" measures straight ahead again after you put the headset back on. The values can be changed by hand under "Fine-tune". The dot is its own small overlay, fixed to the headset 2 m ahead. It only shows while the dashboard is closed. While it is up, the panel waits for each of the compositor's frames (`WaitFrameSync`) and sends a new picture only when it changed.
- The eye fit's measurements count a sample when both eyes read at least 0.3 open (and the gaze is reliable), instead of using `gaze_hold_below` (0.5 by default). A face that reads less open, for example 0.45 when looking down, no longer fails the fit as "unsteady".
- Soft sound cues during the eye fit, so it can be followed without looking at the panel: a pop when a dot is in place, a pip when it's measured, a low buzz when it's measured again, ticks for 3, 2, 1, a chime when you can open your eyes, and a rising chime at the end (falling tones if it stops). "♪ Sounds" on the tab turns them off (`fit_sounds`). They're synthesized at start into `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/frameeyeosc/sounds/` and played with `pw-play` (or `paplay` / `aplay`); nothing changes the system volume.
- Gaze: new settings for where "straight ahead" is and how far the gaze moves from there: `gaze_offset_x` / `gaze_offset_y`, and `gaze_gain_x`, `gaze_gain_up` and `gaze_gain_down`. They apply to both eyes and the combined gaze, before smoothing.
- Eyelids: each eye's openness with the eyes shut and while looking up, straight ahead and down (`lid_fit_*`). The Frame reads an eye as less open when you look down, so a fitted eye is judged against what is normal for where you look and no longer closes when you only look down. Fitted eyes don't use the learned calibration or `lid_scale_*`. On a recording, the times an eye looking down was sent a third closed went from 35 to 5, and more blinks were sent fully closed (53 of 60, from 50).
- Each eye's sideways gaze is fitted too (`gaze_offset_x_left/right`, `gaze_gain_x_left/right`), for "Move eyes separately". The fit aims each eye at the angle it really has to turn to see a dot 2 m away, using the distance between the eyes from SteamVR (63 mm if it doesn't say), so the avatar's eyes turn in naturally. The combined gaze keeps its own values. The "Jittery on the Frame" note on that switch is gone: each eye's gaze is only about 15% less steady than the combined one.
- Looking far down, the Frame's sideways gaze jumps (about 19° to the right from about 30° down), so the avatar looked down and to the right. Below `gaze_down_hold_x_deg` (24° by default; the tracker's own angle) the sideways gaze now fades into its value from just before, fully held 10° further down. On a recording, the sideways gaze sent while looking 32° or more down went from 10.1° to 1.3° (median). It's under "Fine-tune" on the Eye fit tab.
- Debug: "Show gaze dots" on the Advanced tab (`gaze_debug_dots`) shows a small dot 2 m ahead where the gaze being sent points, or one per eye (left cyan, right orange), each from its own eye, with "Move eyes separately". frameeyeosc passes each processed sample to the panel over a Unix socket in its status folder while it's on; nothing leaves the headset. The panel only moves the dots (their images are drawn once). While the dashboard is open they come 0.3 m nearer than the panel (0.5 to 2 m, same apparent size), so it doesn't hide them.
- frameeyeosc averages the raw gaze and each eye's openness when the panel asks (`gaze_capture` in `config.json`, with how long and how much of the start to skip), and reports the result in the status file and as one line in the journal. It now checks `config.json` 10 times a second (a file stat; it is only read when it changed), so a capture starts right away.
- `--replay` also reports the eyelid while looking down and the times it was sent closing, the sideways gaze while looking far down, each eye's jitter and left minus right while fixating, and reads a lid fit from a recording that has one.
- With the defaults (nothing fitted), the output is the same as 0.4.0's, except while looking more than 24° down.

## 0.4.0 (2026-09-27)

Steadier eyes and fewer missed blinks. The ideas come from the README of [CyrusOtter/SteamFrameEye](https://github.com/CyrusOtter/SteamFrameEye).

- A closed eye is sent fully closed for at least 80 ms (`blink_hold_ms`), so short blinks show up for other players. Longer blinks keep their own length.
- One-sample glitches in gaze and eyelids are removed (`despike`). Everything arrives one sample (~11 ms) later.
- When one eye is closed and the other nearly so, both are sent closed (`blink_sync_below`). Winks pass through.
- Optional, off by default: an eye whose gaze the Frame itself marks as uncertain (`gaze_quality_limit`) can be kept from moving the gaze. The other eye moves both, or the gaze is held while both are uncertain, and eyelid calibration doesn't learn meanwhile. It's a safety net. On a well-fitted headset it measured no benefit, because the uncertainty only rises while the eyes are mostly shut, where the gaze is already held.
- The panel has the four new settings: the quality check and glitch removal on the Gaze page, and the blink hold and closing both eyes in one row on the Eyelids page.
- The gaze deadzone default is now 0.02 (0.9°) instead of 0.03 (1.35°), so small gaze shifts come through.
- Gaps in eye tracking are logged to the journal: one line when samples stop, with the reason (the eye server stopped producing, no new samples for 1 s, or an unreadable sample), and one when they come back ("Eye tracking resumed after 5.2 s"). When the eye tracker restarts and re-creates its shared memory, frameeyeosc reattaches and logs it. If the new one isn't ready yet, it keeps retrying instead of exiting.
- `--record FILE` writes the eye tracker's raw samples to a CSV file, and `--replay FILE` runs such a file through the processing and prints how the output behaved, next to the same settings with the steps above turned off.
- The panel shows the installed version on the Advanced page and can update frameeyeosc: it asks GitHub for a newer release at start and at most once a day ("Check for updates", `update_check`, on by default), "Check now" asks right away, and "Update" installs the new release after one confirmation. Downloads are checked against the release's `SHA256SUMS`, a checksum from the same release (not a signature) that catches a corrupted or incomplete download. Releases without it are left for a manual update. After a failed check, the panel tries again an hour later. While a new release is available, a notice at the bottom of the left column leads to it.
- `install.sh` also installs the update script (`~/.local/share/frameeyeosc/frame-update.sh`) and remembers its options in `~/.config/frameeyeosc/install-args`, so an update from the panel installs the same way. Versions before 0.4.0 have no updater, so update to 0.4.0 once by hand with `./install.sh --with-panel` as before. From 0.4.0 on, the panel's "Update" button does it. If `install-args` is missing, the panel's update installs with `--with-panel`.
- `scripts/package.sh` writes `dist/SHA256SUMS` next to the tarball; attach both to the release.

## 0.3.0 (2026-09-27)

## 0.2.0 (2026-09-26)

First release of this fork, based on [konsti219/frameeyeosc](https://github.com/konsti219/frameeyeosc) at `b9f0c01`.

- OSC now goes to the PC that Steam Link is streaming from. `--target auto` is the new default; it used to be `127.0.0.1:9000`.
- Gaze and eyelids go through One Euro filters. Gaze also has a small deadzone and stays put while the eyes are shut.
- Both eyes get the same gaze by default. Use `--independent-eyes` for per-eye gaze.
- Eyelid openness is converted to the VRCFT scale (closed, relaxed, widened), with a deadzone so widening doesn't trigger too easily.
- Each eye's relaxed openness is learned and saved, so small left/right differences even out. Winks still come through.
- It can run as a systemd user service that starts with SteamVR and restarts if it fails. When the eye tracker restarts, it reopens the shared memory.
- Added `install.sh`, which needs no sudo, and `scripts/package.sh` for building release tarballs.
